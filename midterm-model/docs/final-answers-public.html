美国选民右移三问：首投族、学历、族裔

Three Questions on America's Rightward Shift: First-time Voters, Education, Ethnicity

这是一份只说数据能证明的话的报告。每个数字都来自公开选举结果和公开调查，可以复核；说不准的地方，我们明说"说不准"，并告诉你要等什么证据。

This report says only what the data can prove. Every number comes from public election returns and public surveys and can be re-checked. Where the answer is unknowable, we say so — and tell you what evidence to wait for.

一页看懂 · THE SHORT VERSION

  1. 首投族不决定选举。每次选举的摆动主要来自"老选民改主意"，新选民的贡献通常不到 0.5 个百分点。真正最强的信号是全国整体民意（"你更想让哪个党控制国会？"）。
  2. First-time voters do not decide elections. Swings come mostly from existing voters changing their minds; new voters typically add less than half a point. The single strongest signal is the national mood ("which party do you want to control Congress?").
  3. 右移是真的，而且很大。西语裔聚居县的共和党得票率从 2012 年的 31% 升到 2024 年的 50%；低学历密集县从 63% 升到 80%。亚裔也右移了，幅度约为西语裔的一半。机制是"换了党派标签"，不是"变得更保守"。
  4. The shift is real and large. In heavily Hispanic counties the Republican share rose from 31% (2012) to 50% (2024); in low-education counties from 63% to 80%. Asian Americans moved too, about half as far. The mechanism is a change of party label, not a change in ideology.
  5. 永久吗？没人能现在回答。但我们能夹出范围：大部分是"这几年的风向"，可以退潮；可新一代选民一进场就比上一代红得多，这条底线回不去了。2026 年 11 月的中期选举是一次真实检验，我们已经提前写下了判定标准。
  6. 焦虑是真的，出口是空的。租客和无保险者的经济焦虑独立于党派而存在，年轻人想要政府做更多（对政府医保的支持 2012→2024 从 42% 升到 63%）；但这些没有变成选票——最大的未被吸收的池子在共和党内部的租客身上，而没有候选人去接。资源不是瓶颈，参选才是。而这个池子本身正在退出选民体：租房的共和党人核验投票率从 2012 年的 61% 降到 2024 年的 37%。
  7. The anxiety is real; the outlet is empty. Renters' and the uninsured's economic anxiety exists independently of party, and young people want more from government (support for government health insurance rose from 42% to 63%, 2012→2024); none of it has become votes — the largest unabsorbed pool is Republican renters, and no candidate is reaching for them. Money is not the bottleneck; entering the race is. And the pool itself is leaving the electorate: validated turnout among Republican renters fell from 61% in 2012 to 37% in 2024.
  8. Permanent? Nobody can say yet. But we can bound it: most of it is a period wave that can recede, yet each new cohort now enters far more Republican than the last — that floor won't move back. The November 2026 midterm is a real test, and we have written down the pass/fail rules in advance.

问一什么在决定选举结果？首投族吗？

Q1What actually decides elections? Is it first-time voters?

答案 · ANSWER

不是。把 2006 到 2024 年的九次选举逐一拆开看，每一次的变化都主要来自已有选民改变投票；首次投票的人贡献通常不到 0.5 个百分点，最多一次也只有 1.5 个百分点。但首投族本身确实变了：2024 年的首次投票者比八年前红了 10 到 14 个百分点。至于什么最能解释结果——是全国民意：全国"想让哪个党控制国会"的差距每变化 1 个点，各州参议院选举的差距平均变化约 1.3 个点，这一关系从 1998 年到 2022 年稳定成立。

No. Taking the nine elections from 2006 to 2024 one by one, each change came mainly from existing voters switching; first-time voters typically contributed under half a point, at most 1.5 points once. But first-time voters themselves did change: the 2024 cohort was 10–14 points more Republican than eight years earlier. What explains outcomes best is the national mood: for every 1-point move in the national "which party should control Congress" gap, state Senate races move about 1.3 points — a relationship stable from 1998 to 2022.

全国民意是最强信号

National mood is the strongest signal

系数约 1.33，在不同年份子集里都在 1.24–1.34 之间、方向一致；把它加进模型后，对没见过的新选举年的预测误差明显下降。

Coefficient ≈1.33, between 1.24 and 1.34 across year subsets, always the same sign; adding it clearly reduces error on election years the model had never seen.

"执政党中期挨罚"——通常成立，2022 例外

"The president's party loses midterms" — usually, but not in 2022

这条老规律在数据里很稳，却在 2022 年反了向。全国民意读数当年恰好"感知"到了这个反常，纯规律做不到。

The old rule is stable in the data but reversed in 2022. The national-mood reading picked up that anomaly; the rule alone could not.

谁去投票，差别很大

Who turns out matters a lot

2022 年大学以上学历者投票率 78.7%，高中及以下只有 50.5%，差 28 个百分点；18–24 岁投票率 2014→2018→2022 为 22%→43%→35%。

In 2022, turnout was 78.7% for college graduates vs 50.5% for high-school-or-less — a 28-point gap; ages 18–24 went 22%→43%→35% across 2014→2018→2022.

首投族假说：强版本否定，弱版本成立

First-voter hypothesis: strong form rejected, weak form holds

"首投族决定结果"——否定。"首投族在变红"——成立，且和"新一代入场就更红"的发现互相印证（见问三）。

"First-time voters decide elections" — rejected. "First-time voters are turning red" — confirmed, consistent with new cohorts entering more Republican (see Q3).

问二低学历、西语裔、亚裔选民右移了多少？

Q2How far have low-education, Hispanic and Asian voters moved right?

答案 · ANSWER

很多。在西语裔占七成以上的约 30 个县里，共和党在两党票中的份额：2012 年 30.8% → 2016 年 29.7% → 2020 年 39.3% → 2024 年 49.6%，八年上升近 20 个百分点，2020 年起加速。在高中及以下学历人口密集的县：62.8% → 80.3%，一路上升。这两组是直接从县级选举结果里读出来的，几乎不需要假设。亚裔没有这样的聚居县，改用一项覆盖 71.9 万受访者的全国调查：亚裔在众议院投票中的共和党份额 2018 年 27.4% → 2024 年 36.3%——右移真实，幅度约为西语裔的一半，2022 到 2024 年趋平。

A lot. In the ~30 counties that are at least 70% Hispanic, the Republican share of the two-party vote went 30.8% (2012) → 29.7% (2016) → 39.3% (2020) → 49.6% (2024) — nearly 20 points in eight years, accelerating after 2020. In counties dense with high-school-or-less adults: 62.8% → 80.3%, rising every cycle. Both are read directly from county election returns and need almost no assumptions. Asian Americans have no such counties, so we use a national survey of 719,000 respondents: their Republican share in House voting rose from 27.4% (2018) to 36.3% (2024) — real, about half the Hispanic move, and flattening from 2022 to 2024.

是怎么右移的：换了党派标签，没有变得更保守

How the shift happened: a change of party label, not of ideology

我们把 2016→2024 的变化拆成两部分：一是"人群构成变了"（比如更多人自称共和党人），二是"同样的人投票不同了"。按党派认同拆，构成变化占了西语裔变化的一半以上（+7.8 到 8.3 个百分点），亚裔和低学历群体也一样；按意识形态拆，构成几乎没变。最直接的证据：西语裔选民里自称保守派的比例八年纹丝不动（29.7% → 29.7%），自称自由派的反而上升（25.6% → 32.6%），但自称共和党人的比例从 23.5% 升到 33.7%，自称民主党人的从 66.7% 降到 55.3%。同样的人，换了党标签，然后照着标签投票。历史上这种"重新认同"比"一时叛离"更持久——这是问三的重要线索。

We split the 2016→2024 change into two parts: "the makeup of the group changed" (e.g. more people now call themselves Republicans) versus "the same people vote differently". Split by party identification, composition explains over half the Hispanic change (+7.8 to 8.3 points), and likewise for Asian and low-education voters; split by ideology, composition barely moved. The most direct evidence: the share of Hispanic voters calling themselves conservative did not budge in eight years (29.7% → 29.7%), self-described liberals actually rose (25.6% → 32.6%), yet self-identified Republicans rose from 23.5% to 33.7% and Democrats fell from 66.7% to 55.3%. Same people, new party label, voting along the label. Historically such re-identification is stickier than a one-off defection — an important clue for Q3.

问三这次右移是永久的吗？

Q3Is the shift permanent?

答案 · ANSWER

从过去的数据原则上回答不了"永久"——凡是声称能回答的模型都越界了。能做的是把范围夹紧。看不同出生年代的西语裔选民：2018→2024 年，每一个年龄段都同步右移了 10 到 25 个百分点（1980 年后出生的最猛，23% → 51%）。这说明右移主要是"这几年的风向"，不是"老一代被新一代替换"——风向可以退。但有一条底线回不去了：2000 年后出生的选民第一次投票就有 34–40% 投共和党，过去这个年龄段只有 17–24%。再加上问二的发现（换标签比叛离更持久），我们的判断是"部分粘性"：能退一部分，退不回 2016。

"Permanent" cannot be answered from past data in principle — any model claiming otherwise is overreaching. What we can do is tighten the bounds. Looking at Hispanic voters by decade of birth: from 2018 to 2024, every age group moved right in step, by 10–25 points (those born after 1980 most sharply, 23% → 51%). That says the shift is mostly a period wave, not generational replacement — and waves can recede. But one floor has moved for good: voters born after 2000 cast 34–40% of their first votes Republican, where that age group used to be 17–24%. Add the Q2 finding (re-labelling is stickier than defection) and our judgement is partly sticky: some can recede, but not back to 2016.

2026 年 11 月：提前写好的检验

November 2026: a test written down in advance

为了不让自己事后"看图说话"，我们在选举前就冻结了检验规则：县名单固定、统计口径固定、及格线固定，任何人都可以在 2026 年 11 月之后用同一份公开数据重算。规则：把 2026 年众议院选举里这些县的共和党份额，和同一批县 2022 年的份额比——只跌 2 个百分点以内算"粘住了"，跌 7 个百分点以上算"退潮了"，中间按比例算部分粘性。2022 年的基准线已经算出来并封存：

一句话 · BOTTOM LINE

即使按现在对民主党最有利的民调，民主党也只到约 49 席，够不着 51。要翻转参议院，民主党得在改选的 33 席里拿下 17 席——守住自己的 13 席再抢 4 席——而按现读数机械换算只有约 15 席。所以民调偏民主党的"风"，更可能体现为众议院翻转和参议院席位差缩小，而不是参议院易手。

Even on today's most Democrat-friendly reading, Democrats reach only about 49 seats — short of 51. To flip the Senate they would need 17 of the 33 seats on the ballot — hold all 13 of their own and take 4 more — while the mechanical conversion of the current reading gives about 15. So the pro-Democratic "wind" is more likely to show up as a House flip and a narrower Senate margin than as a Senate takeover.

One more headwind: the voters Republicans gained in 2024 (low-education, Hispanic) are exactly the groups with the lowest midterm turnout.

通往 51 席的路 · THE PATH TO 51

民主党的算术：不改选的 34 席 + 守住自己改选的 13 席 = 47；再拿下评级共识偏向他们的北卡罗来纳和缅因 = 49；最后两席必须来自四个掷硬币席位（俄亥俄补选、阿拉斯加、艾奥瓦、得克萨斯）——而这四个州的底色都是共和党领先 5 到 14 个百分点。这就是为什么全国民意 D+6 的风，落到参议院只能"缩小差距"：风要吹过 51，需要在四个红色州里赢两个，而且守方一席不能丢。反过来，共和党的风险集中在两点：北卡罗来纳的开放席位，和缅因现任能否再一次超越州底色。

The Democratic arithmetic: 34 seats not up + holding all 13 of their own on the ballot = 47; add North Carolina and Maine, where rater consensus favours them = 49; the last two must come from the four coin flips (Ohio special, Alaska, Iowa, Texas) — all states Republicans carried by 5 to 14 points in 2024. That is why a D+6 national wind only narrows the Senate: to reach 51 it must win two of four red states without dropping a single defensive seat. Republican risk, conversely, concentrates in two places: the open seat in North Carolina, and whether Maine's incumbent can outrun the state's lean one more time.

检验三个流行叙事，放到数据里会怎样

TESTSThree popular narratives, checked against the data

关于美国选民，最近有三种说法流传很广：年轻人男女正在分道扬镳；经济焦虑在把人推向极端；民粹情绪左右轮流得势。我们用同一套公开调查（71.9 万受访者，2006–2024）逐条检验，事先写下"如果说法成立应该看到什么"，再看数据。三条里，只有一条部分成立。

Three claims about American voters circulate widely: young men and women are drifting apart; economic anxiety is pushing people to extremes; populist moods surge left and right in turn. We tested each against the same public survey (719,000 respondents, 2006–2024), writing down beforehand what we should see if the claim were true. Of the three, only one partly holds.

1 · 年轻人的性别分化：女性在左移，男性没怎么动

1 · The youth gender divide: women moving left, men mostly not moving

结果 · FINDING

投票上的分化比传说小得多。18–29 岁女性与男性在众议院投票中的民主党份额差，2006 年以来一直在 0 到 6 个百分点之间：2018 年 6.4、2022 年 5.7，2024 年回落到 2.4。真正变了的是自我认同：自称"非常自由派"的年轻女性从 2016 年的 12.6% 升到 2024 年的 22.0%，而年轻男性自称保守派的比例几乎没动（28.2% → 28.5%）。所以在美国数据里，这是女性左移，不是男性右移——而且还没有充分转化为投票差。它是年龄现象还是世代现象，两次同年龄段对比一正一负，目前判断不了。

The divide at the ballot box is far smaller than the story. Among 18–29-year-olds, the gap between women's and men's Democratic share of the House vote has stayed between 0 and 6 points since 2006: 6.4 in 2018, 5.7 in 2022, back to 2.4 in 2024. What did change is self-description: young women calling themselves "very liberal" rose from 12.6% (2016) to 22.0% (2024), while young men calling themselves conservative barely moved (28.2% → 28.5%). In U.S. data this is women moving left, not men moving right — and it has not yet turned into much of a voting gap. Whether it is an age or a generational effect cannot be settled: the two same-age comparisons point in opposite directions.

2 · 经济焦虑：更像党派立场的回声，不是独立的推力

2 · Economic anxiety: an echo of party loyalty more than an independent push

结果 · FINDING

问受访者"过去一年国家经济变好还是变坏"，答案越来越取决于你是不是总统所在党的人：总统党内与党外的悲观率差距，2008 年 10 个百分点，2020 年 45 个，2024 年 58 个。悲观者的投票也不是一贯偏向某一方——民主党执政时悲观者更投共和党，共和党执政时反过来——这是"回声"的签名，不是独立因子的签名。另一方面，把收入和就业状况控制住之后，2016 到 2024 年"说经济变差"的比例仍然多了 23 个百分点：主观焦虑正在脱离客观处境。含义：经济焦虑会放大摆动，但在美国选票数据里它本身不是触发器。至于"人们对资本主义失去耐心"，这套调查没有问，我们不假装测到了。

Asked whether the national economy got better or worse over the past year, people increasingly answer by whether the president is from their party: the pessimism gap between the president's party and the other party was 10 points in 2008, 45 in 2020, 58 in 2024. Nor do pessimists consistently vote one way — under a Democratic president they lean Republican, under a Republican president the reverse — the signature of an echo, not of an independent factor. Yet after controlling for income and employment, the share saying the economy got worse still rose 23 points from 2016 to 2024: felt anxiety is detaching from actual circumstances. Meaning: economic anxiety amplifies swings, but in U.S. vote data it is not itself a trigger. As for "losing patience with capitalism", the survey never asks — and we do not pretend to have measured it.

3 · 民粹轮替：摆动在收敛，本党不满没有上升

3 · Populist turnover: swings are shrinking, and in-party discontent is not rising

结果 · FINDING

如果选民真的在左右民粹之间来回摆，应该看到两件事：每届选举里"老选民改主意"的幅度越来越大，以及对自己党的总统越来越不满。数据两条都不支持：近四届的摆动幅度平均 5.8 个百分点，低于之前五届的 8.5；2018 和 2022 年对本党总统的不满率也低于 2010 和 2014 年。把这和前面的发现放在一起看，美国这轮右移更像"换党派标签、跟着党派看经济"的重组，而不是对整个建制的双向反叛。至于"左右轮替是一种周期"——十次选举从原则上就识别不了周期，我们不替数据下这种结论。

If voters really swung between left and right populism, two things should show: bigger and bigger mind-changing swings each election, and rising discontent with one's own party's president. The data support neither: the average swing over the last four elections was 5.8 points, down from 8.5 over the previous five; in-party disapproval in 2018 and 2022 was also lower than in 2010 and 2014. Read together with the findings above, this rightward shift looks like re-sorting — new party labels, economy judged through the party lens — rather than a two-sided revolt against the establishment. As for "left-right turnover as a cycle": ten elections cannot identify a cycle in principle, and we will not draw that conclusion for the data.

候选人候选人本身有多重要？进步派在偏红区吃亏吗？

CANDIDATESHow much does the candidate matter? Do progressives lose ground in red districts?

前面说全国民意是最强的因子。那候选人呢？我们的度量是"超额表现"：把一个选区或州的结果，减去它的党派底色和当年的全国风向，剩下的就是候选人（及其对手、筹款、知名度）带来的部分。用 2018–2024 四届众议院 1,500 个选区和 2026 年 11 个参议院竞争州来算。

The national mood is the strongest factor. What about candidates? Our yardstick is "over-performance": take a district's or state's result, subtract its partisan lean and that year's national wind, and what remains is the candidate's share (plus opponent, money, name recognition). Computed on ~1,500 House districts across 2018–2024 and 11 competitive 2026 Senate states.

结果 · FINDING

候选人是偏红区的门票，全国风向是场馆开不开门。众议院：现任比挑战者多约 3 个百分点；一个候选人一般能在底色之外挪动 ±3–4 个点。参议院比众议院"个人化"得多——州级参议院民调只吸收总统底色的约四分之一（众议院几乎是 1:1），所以个别候选人能做出 +7（北卡罗来纳开放席位）这样的差距。但即便如此，候选人效应的量级仍在全国风向之下：风向每变 1 个点，各州跟着变 1.3 个点，而且所有州一样，摇摆州并没有被"放大"。

The candidate is the ticket into a red district; the national wind decides whether the venue is open. House: incumbents run about 3 points ahead of challengers, and a candidate typically moves a result ±3–4 points beyond the district's lean. The Senate is far more personal — state Senate polls absorb only about a quarter of the presidential lean (the House is nearly 1:1), so an individual can open gaps like +7 (North Carolina's open seat). Even so, candidate effects sit below the national wind: each 1-point shift in national mood moves every state by about 1.3 points, uniformly — swing states are not "amplified".

进步派在偏红区：没被证实吃亏，但几乎不参选

Progressives in red districts: no proven penalty — they mostly don't run

我们用公开背书（Justice Democrats、Our Revolution、Working Families Party、Sanders、Warren、Ocasio-Cortez 任一背书）作为透明的"进步派"标签，不做主观判断。四届众议院里，偏红选区（R+5 以上）的民主党候选人中带这个标签的只有 46 人（对照组 600 多人）；2026 年 11 个参议院竞争州里只有 1 人。数据能说的三件事：

We use public endorsements (any of Justice Democrats, Our Revolution, Working Families Party, Sanders, Warren, Ocasio-Cortez) as a transparent "progressive" label — no subjective coding. Across four House cycles, only 46 Democratic candidates in red districts (R+5 or more) carried the label (against 600-plus without); in the 11 competitive 2026 Senate states, one. Three things the data can say:

  • 供给稀缺是硬事实。偏红区有竞争的民主党初选里，出现进步派参选者的比例 2018 年约 9–29%（R+15 以上只有 9%），2020 年 9–18%，2022 年竞争性初选本身塌缩（R+5–10 仅 9 场）。进步派在民主党大浪年才进偏红区，退潮就撤。
  • Scarce supply is the hard fact. Among contested Democratic primaries in red districts, the share with a progressive entrant was roughly 9–29% in 2018 (only 9% at R+15 and beyond), 9–18% in 2020, and in 2022 contested primaries themselves collapsed (just 9 at R+5–10). Progressives enter red districts in Democratic wave years and withdraw when the tide goes out.
  • 参选的那些表现并不差。四届合计，偏红区进步派的超额表现中位比非进步派高约 3.8 个百分点，2018 年最明显。但这是选择效应的签名——他们挑有利年份、挑自认能赢的区参选——不能读成"进步派立场更能赢"。
  • Those who do run don't do badly. Pooled over four cycles, progressives in red districts over-performed non-progressives by a median of about 3.8 points, most visibly in 2018. But that is the signature of selection — they pick favourable years and winnable districts — not evidence that a progressive platform wins more.
  • "初选选出更激进的人，大选就要付出代价"——政治学的经典结果（Hall 2015：9–13 个百分点）。我们在 2018–2022 的势均力敌初选里只找到 17 场可比对决，进步派胜出组的大选表现比对照低约 3.7 个点：方向一致，幅度远小于经典估计，但样本太小，只报不判。
  • "Nominate the more radical candidate and pay in November" — the classic political-science result (Hall 2015: 9–13 points). In 2018–2022 we found only 17 comparable near-tie primaries; where the progressive won, general-election performance was about 3.7 points lower: same direction, far smaller than the classic estimate, but too few cases to judge — reported, not ruled.

一句话：进步派要在偏红区获得权力，第一个约束不是"候选人不够硬"，而是"根本没人去参选"；第二个约束是全国风向。至于进步派标签本身在偏红区是资产还是负债，现有数据既没证实也没证伪。

In one line: for progressives to win power in red districts, the first constraint is not candidate strength but the absence of candidates; the second is the national wind. Whether the progressive label itself is an asset or a liability there, the data neither confirm nor refute.

焦虑真实的焦虑，缺席的出口：住房、医保、再分配

ANXIETYReal anxieties, missing outlets: housing, health care, redistribution

年轻人的经济焦虑是真的吗？他们想要什么？政治为什么不回应？我们把这个问题拆成三层，分别用不同的公开数据回答：处境（租房还是有房、有没有医保、有没有资产，71.9 万人，2010–2024）、态度（政府该不该管医保、保障收入、增加服务，美国全国选举研究 1972–2024；以及 2024 年对住房、Medicaid、税收具体政策的支持，6 万人）、投票（同一批人怎么投）。三层对不上，就是答案。

Is young people's economic anxiety real? What do they want? Why doesn't politics respond? We split this into three layers, each answered from different public data: circumstances (renting vs owning, insured or not, assets, 719,000 people, 2010–2024), attitudes (should government run health insurance, guarantee incomes, provide more services — American National Election Studies 1972–2024; plus 2024 support for specific housing, Medicaid and tax policies, 60,000 people), and votes (how the same people vote). Where the layers fail to line up is the answer.

结果 · FINDING

焦虑是真的，而且独立于党派；态度上想要更多政府；但这两者都没有变成投票。扣掉党派"回声"后，租客说经济变差的比例仍高出 5 个百分点，无保险者高出 8 个点。18–29 岁支持政府医保的比例从 2012 年的 42% 升到 2024 年的 63%，支持政府保障就业与收入的从 40% 升到 55%；年轻人与 65 岁以上的差距从 1972 年的 −8 个点变成 2024 年的 +23 个点——代际分歧是新的，主要出现在 2016 年之后。可是在投票上，同一党内的租客和房主投得几乎一样（差不到 2 个点），无保险者的民主党份额反而从 50% 降到 41%。

The anxiety is real and independent of party; attitudes lean toward more government; neither has become a vote. Net of the partisan "echo", renters are 5 points more likely to say the economy got worse, the uninsured 8 points. Among 18–29-year-olds, support for government health insurance rose from 42% (2012) to 63% (2024), and for government-guaranteed jobs and income from 40% to 55%; the gap between the young and those 65+ went from −8 points in 1972 to +23 in 2024 — the generational divide is new, and mostly post-2016. Yet at the ballot box, renters and owners within the same party vote almost identically (under 2 points apart), and the uninsured's Democratic share actually fell from 50% to 41%.

为什么政治不回应——不是没钱，是没人去

Why politics doesn't respond — not for lack of money, for lack of entrants

  • 资源不是瓶颈。用联邦选举委员会的候选人财务数据（2018–2026）核对：在偏红选区参选的进步派民主党候选人，个人捐款中位数约 144 万美元，是非进步派的 6.7 倍；PAC 捐款也更多。党委员会的直接转入对两类都可以忽略。（匹配率 52%，超级 PAC 的外部支出不在数据内。）
  • Money is not the bottleneck. Federal Election Commission candidate finances (2018–2026): progressive Democrats who run in red districts raise a median of about $1.44M in individual contributions — 6.7 times non-progressives — and more PAC money too. Direct party-committee transfers are negligible for both. (Match rate 52%; super-PAC outside spending is not in the data.)
  • 小额捐款也不是。补上联邦选举委员会逐候选人的小额（<200 美元）拆分（2018–2024 偏红区 354 名民主党众院候选人）：进步派的小额占比中位 28% 与非进步派的 30% 相当；但小额绝对量中位 53 万美元 vs 10 万（5 倍），总筹款 381 万 vs 37 万（10 倍）。"草根势头"是体量，不是依赖度——进步派在每个来源上都筹得更多。资金体量与超额表现同向（相关约 0.5），但方向互为因果：强候选人筹得多。
  • Nor is small-dollar money. Adding the FEC's per-candidate split of contributions under $200 (354 Democratic House candidates in red districts, 2018–2024): progressives' small-dollar share (median 28%) matches non-progressives' (30%), but their small-dollar volume is 5× (median $533K vs $104K) and total receipts 10× ($3.8M vs $368K). "Grassroots momentum" is scale, not dependence — progressives raise more from every source. Money scale tracks over-performance (r ≈ 0.5), but the arrow runs both ways: strong candidates raise more.
  • 那"合格的候选人"到底是什么？把 2018–2024 全部 1,499 名民主党众院提名人的可测特征放进同一个模型（按届扣掉选区底色后的超额表现）：唯一在四届、每种切法下都稳定为正的特征，是选举前已有维基百科条目——挑战者 +3.8 个百分点，偏红区 +4.8。"曾任州议员/市长"之类的履历头衔，一旦控制了知名度，贡献为零甚至为负；"进步派"标签控制了知名度和筹款后也转为负（-2.1）——前面看到的进步派 +3.8 是谁被选出来的效应，不是标签本身。筹款每高一个数量级加 1.9 个百分点，并吸收知名度的一部分：知名度和钱互为因果，分不开。"合格"在数据里的形状很朴素：选前就已经是公共人物，且筹得到数量级更多的钱；不是履历，不是意识形态。但这些特征合起来只解释候选人效应 6–17% 的方差——"合格"的大部分仍是看不见的。
  • So what is a "qualified candidate"? Putting every measurable trait of all 1,499 Democratic House nominees (2018–2024) into one model of over-performance net of district lean: the only trait that stays positive in every cycle and every cut is having a Wikipedia article before the election — +3.8 points for challengers, +4.8 in red districts. Résumé titles ("state senator", "mayor") add nothing once name recognition is held fixed — zero or negative; the "progressive" label turns negative too (-2.1) once recognition and money are controlled — the +3.8 seen earlier is about who gets selected, not the label. Each tenfold step in fundraising adds 1.9 points and absorbs part of the recognition effect: fame and money cause each other and can't be separated. In the data, "qualified" has a plain shape: already a public figure before the race, and able to raise an order of magnitude more money; not a résumé, not an ideology. Yet all these traits together explain only 6–17% of the variance in candidate effects — most of what "qualified" means is still invisible.
  • "有维基条目"是不是事后才写上去的？我们查了这 245 位有条目的挑战者的条目创建时间：174 位在选举年之前就有条目，54 位是竞选期间被写进去的，只有 17 位是选后才有。选前就有条目的候选人仍然多赢 3.5 个百分点（偏红区 4.2）——这一档不可能是事后偏差；选后才建条的那一档多赢 2.8，这就是偏差的上限。竞选期间被写进维基的候选人反而最强（4.9，偏红区 8.1；其中进步派占 28%）：竞选中"成为话题"本身是信号。控制筹款后，选前知名度的贡献降到 1.9——知名度主要通过钱起作用。
  • Is "has a Wikipedia article" just written after the fact? We dated the articles of all 245 challengers who have one: 174 existed before the election year, 54 were created during the campaign, only 17 after the election. Candidates whose article predates the election year still over-perform by 3.5 points (4.2 in red districts) — that tier cannot carry hindsight bias; the after-the-fact tier over-performs by 2.8, which bounds the bias. Candidates written up mid-campaign are the strongest of all (4.9; 8.1 in red districts; 28% progressive): becoming a story during the race is itself the signal. Once fundraising is controlled, pre-existing recognition falls to 1.9 — recognition works mainly through money.
  • 那是话题先动，还是钱先动？把这 39 位竞选期间被写进维基的候选人的联邦选举委员会逐期报告拿来看：条目建立后的第一个申报期，日均筹款是建立前最后一期的 2.68 倍；27/39 人的筹款峰值出现在建条之后。按预设规则分类，"话题先动"21 人、"钱先动"12 人、看不出 6 人。但要分季节：9 月前建条的 31 人里钱先动只有 5 人；10 月才建条的 8 人里 7 人是钱先动——冲刺期被写进维基，是对已经飙升的筹款的事后记录。所以"竞选中成为话题"在初选季和夏天是领先信号，到了 10 月就只是滞后信号。回到模型里也一样：9 月前成为话题的候选人，控制筹款后仍多赢 4.0 个百分点；10 月才成为话题的，控制筹款后只剩 1.3，不显著。
  • Does the story come first, or the money? Pulling the FEC period-by-period reports for these 39 candidates written up mid-campaign: in the first filing period after the article appeared, daily fundraising ran 2.68× the last period before it, and 27 of 39 hit their fundraising peak after the article. By pre-set rules, "story first" 21, "money first" 12, indeterminate 6. Season matters: among the 31 written up before September only 5 were money-first; among the 8 written up in October, 7 were — a late article is hindsight on money already surging. So "becoming a story" leads in primary season and summer, and merely lags by October. The model agrees: candidates who became a story before September still over-perform by 4.0 points with fundraising controlled; those written up in October keep only 1.3, not distinguishable from zero.
  • 那"成为话题"是谁触发的？把这 39 位候选人的维基百科日浏览量（选举年 1 月至选举日）拿来看：9 人的条目是在初选前后一周内建立的（其中 7 人恰在初选次日）；初选前就有条目的人里，又有 6 人的第一次注意力尖峰落在初选窗口。合起来 15/39 的"成为话题"是赢初选这个制度事件触发的；初选前就被外界自发关注的只有 3 人。所以"早成为话题"不是媒体先发现了谁，而是初选把谁推到了台前——它独立于钱的那部分效应，本质上是初选胜利带来的知名度。
  • What triggers "becoming a story"? Daily Wikipedia pageviews for these 39 candidates (January of the election year to election day): 9 had their article created within a week of the primary (7 of them the very next day); among those with an earlier article, 6 more saw their first attention spike inside the primary window. Together, 15 of 39 became a story because of one institutional event — winning the primary; only 3 drew outside attention before it. "Becoming a story early" is not the press discovering someone; it is the primary putting them on stage — the part of the effect that is independent of money is, at bottom, the recognition a primary win confers.
  • 供给才是。偏红区有竞争的民主党初选里，进步派参选率 2018 年 9–29%，2022 年塌缩；共和党内的租客池没有任何一方去争取——民主党候选人在偏红区把选票押在"我不是那种民主党人"上，而不是住房与医保。
  • Supply is. In contested Democratic primaries in red districts, progressive entry was 9–29% in 2018 and collapsed by 2022; nobody contests the Republican renter pool — Democratic candidates in red districts bet on "I'm not that kind of Democrat", not on housing and health care.
  • 激励结构解释了这一切。分类完成的选民体里，谈身份的边际收益高于谈方案；方案会得罪自己联盟的某一块，身份不会。方案缺位是均衡，不是失误。
  • The incentive structure explains it. In a fully sorted electorate, identity pays more at the margin than policy; a policy offends part of your own coalition, identity does not. The missing outlet is an equilibrium, not a mistake.

一个诚实的修正：青年对政府医保的支持上升不只发生在民主党年轻人身上（59% → 81%），年轻共和党人也从 22% 升到 37%；但在"政府保障就业与收入"上，年轻共和党人几乎没动（27% → 29%）。医保议题在跨越党派，再分配议题没有。"对资本主义失去耐心"这类问题调查里没有，我们没有测量。

An honest qualification: the rise in youth support for government health insurance is not only among young Democrats (59% → 81%); young Republicans also moved, from 22% to 37%. On government-guaranteed jobs and income, however, young Republicans barely moved (27% → 29%). Health care is crossing party lines; redistribution is not. "Losing patience with capitalism" is not a survey item and was not measured.

出口是谁打开的？2018 年打开过一次——但不是议题，是风向

(2) The outlet opened only in 2018: that year 20% of repeal-opposing Republicans voted Democratic for the House, five times the rate among repeal supporters; by 2024 the same attitude produced only 9%. (3) The first response to cross-pressure is always abstention: ACA-supporting Republicans turn out 15–19 points less than repeal supporters.

我们先给了一个错的解释，然后把它证伪了

We first gave a wrong explanation, then falsified it

第一版解释是"议题在选票上才有出口"——2018 年废除 ACA 是现实威胁。要检验它，需要一个上票时序相反的议题：堕胎。2018 年堕胎不在选票上，2022 年 Dobbs 判决之后 2024 年高度显著。如果解释成立，支持堕胎权的共和党人的脱党差应该 2024 大于 2018。我们把预期先写下来，再看数据：

The expectation failed. Pro-choice Republicans voted Democratic 16% in 2018 and 9% in 2024 (anti-choice: 4.8% → 2.6%): the gap shrank from 11.3 to 6.2, matching the ACA gap's 15.6 → 6.9 — two issues with opposite ballot timing, and the outlet closed for both. The more parsimonious reading: 2018 was a Democratic wave year, and the national wind amplified cross-pressured Republicans' defection; in 2024 the wind leaned Republican and the same cross-pressure showed up only as exit. The wind opens the outlet; the issue decides who is standing at the door. This matches this report's first law (national mood is the strongest factor) — and is one public self-correction.

判决点：2022 年——堕胎在票上，风向却偏共和党

The decisive year: 2022 — abortion on the ballot, wind leaning Republican

The conclusion now stands: the national wind opens the outlet for cross-pressured Republicans; the issue only decides who is standing at the door; when the wind is against them they don't switch, they stay home — especially in midterms. This ties the "anxiety and outlets" section back to this report's first law.

方法怎么读这份报告

METHODHow to read this report

  • "解释"和"预测"是两回事，我们分开放。一个机制可以在历史上很稳定却在下一次失灵（2022 年的"执政党挨罚"就是例子）。所以解释性的结论（问一到问三）和 2026 年的观察读数分开呈现，互不背书。
  • Explaining and forecasting are different jobs, kept apart. A mechanism can be stable in history and fail next time (2022's "punish the president's party" is the example). So the explanatory findings (Q1–Q3) and the 2026 readings are shown separately and do not vouch for each other.
  • 宽而诚实的范围，和准但脆弱的点估计，我们并排给。灰色条不依赖任何行为假设，所以宽；红点依赖"住在这里≈在这里投票"，所以准。
  • Wide-but-honest ranges and precise-but-fragile point estimates are shown side by side. The grey bars assume nothing about behaviour, so they are wide; the red dots assume "lives here ≈ votes here", so they are precise.
  • 检验规则先冻结，再看结果。2026 年的判定标准、县名单、基准线都在选举前公开封存，事后不能改。
  • Rules are frozen before results arrive. The 2026 pass/fail lines, county list and baseline were sealed publicly before the election and cannot be changed afterwards.
